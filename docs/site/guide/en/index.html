Quick start

For the impatient: your transceiver in the browser in a quarter of an hour. The rest of the guide explains each step in detail; come back to it at the first problem.

You need

  • a transceiver known to Hamlib, connected to the PC: a USB cable for most recent transceivers (control and audio), otherwise an interface such as Digirig or SignaLink;
  • a PC running Debian 12 or 13 or Windows 10 / 11 64-bit, or a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 (Raspberry Pi OS 64-bit, official power supply), always on near the transceiver, connected to the internet box;
  • a dummy load for the first transmission;
  • a phone or a PC with a recent browser.

0. First, the transceiver TOT (2 min)

Safety. The TOT (Time Out Timer) is the timer that cuts the transmission after a maximum time. Set the transceiver's one before anything else.

Why? Remotely, you cannot see your transceiver. If the station PC freezes, if the USB cable comes loose or if a program crashes during a transmission, nobody can put the transceiver back to receive any more: it would keep transmitting a carrier, block the frequency, heat up, and you would remain responsible.

What it is for. The station has its own TOT (90 s by default) and a supervisor that watches it. The transceiver TOT is the last barrier: it is inside the transceiver itself, it cuts even when the PC, the cable or the station has failed.

How. In the transceiver menu, look for “Time-Out Timer” (“Time-Out Timer (CI-V)” on Icom) or “TX TIME OUT TIMER” and choose 3 minutes: longer than the station TOT, it never cuts a normal transmission.

The menu path for your brand is in the table below. On the IC-7300MK2, the station reads this setting back at each connection and warns you if it is OFF.

0. Before you start

What you will get

  • Your transceiver controlled from a browser (PC, tablet, phone) or the Android app: frequency, mode, filters, S-meter, received audio, and voice transmission with the phone's or the PC's microphone.
  • A station that protects itself: it always starts in receive, cuts the transmission after a maximum duration (90 s by default), if the phone stops answering or if the link drops, and refuses to transmit outside the bands you have declared.
  • An independent supervisor that watches the station and can send you alerts on your phone.

How it is organised

  • You use a browser or the Android app. They talk to the gateway, the heart of the station, which runs on the station PC next to the transceiver.
  • The gateway controls the transceiver through rigctld (the Hamlib program) and exchanges audio with the transceiver's sound card. It is the gateway that decides on transmission and applies the safety rules.
  • The supervisor watches everything independently: it can only cut a transmission, restart a service, and send you alerts.
  • openRemoteTRX Console, the dashboard and the indicator are your tools on the PC: they read the station's status.
  • The configuration fits in a single file, station.toml.

Each word is explained in the glossary.

1. Prepare the transceiver

Station switched off, nothing connected: now is the time to set up the transceiver. The details of your transceiver's menus are in its sheet (Icom IC-7300MK2, Yaesu FTDX10); for another transceiver, see its manual and the “Depending on your brand” table of the quick start. This part tells you in which order to do things.

1.1 Install the USB driver (Windows only)

On Windows, the transceiver's USB driver (the one from its manufacturer: “Icom USB Driver”, Silicon Labs CP210x for Yaesu…) is installed before you plug in the USB cable; otherwise Windows may install the wrong driver. The name, the minimum version and the procedure are in the transceiver sheet, “USB driver”, or else on the manufacturer's website.

On Debian and on a Raspberry Pi, there is nothing to install: the kernel recognises the transceiver by itself.

1.2 Connect

  1. Antenna disconnected, dummy load on the antenna socket in use.
  2. USB cable between the transceiver and the PC.
  3. Switch the transceiver on (long press on ON/OFF).

The PC now sees one or two serial ports and a sound card. The transceiver sheet tells you which one is used for control (IC-7300MK2: the first one, the second one is used for the HF spectrum; FTDX10: the “Enhanced COM Port”, the “Standard” one is not used).

Debian

Nothing to note: the wizard finds the right port by itself. To see it: ls -l /dev/serial/by-id/ in a terminal (one stable name per port, which does not change from one USB socket to another).

Raspberry Pi

As on Debian. Plug the transceiver cable directly into a USB port of the Pi (the blue USB 3 ports work too); use a hub only if it has its own power supply: the Pi does not supply enough current to several devices.

Windows

Device Manager > Ports (COM & LPT): note the COM number of the control port.

1.3 Set the transceiver menus

Set the menus listed in your transceiver's sheet, section “Menus to set” (otherwise, its manual). They are essential:

  • CAT speed identical to the station's (chosen in the wizard);
  • voice over USB: otherwise the transceiver modulates with its own microphone, that is, the noise of the room. On an Icom (IC-7300MK2): MENU > SET > Connectors > MOD Input > DATA MOD = USB (the station only transmits in data mode); the transceiver accepts the USB input only in DATA MOD or DATA OFF MOD. With the second port set up, the station checks it at each connection and warns you if it is not on USB;
  • no RTS/DTR line for PTT or CW: otherwise the transceiver may go into transmit when the PC starts;
  • VOX off: a VOX that is on makes the transceiver transmit on a noise in the room, without any command. The station checks it each time an operator connects and turns it off if it is on (message in the page log, code ORT-5034). On some transceivers it stays on in one mode while it is off in another (seen on the IC-7300MK2: off in data mode, still on in voice);
  • built-in antenna tuner (if the antenna goes through it): tuning must start by itself when transmission is keyed on a new frequency. On the IC-7300MK2: MENU > SET > Function > Tuner, [TUNER] Switch = Auto (tuner state stored per band) and PTT Start = ON. Answer “yes” to the wizard's tuner question: the station will check these settings at each connection and warn you if they are wrong (it never changes them). Outside the amateur bands, the transceiver does not guarantee tuning on PTT. During a transmission, if the SWR exceeds 2 for more than one second, the page shows a red banner and beeps (it does not cut);
  • transceiver transmit timer (TX TIME OUT TIMER): a second safety, independent of the station. Set it (3 min recommended): if the station, the network or the PC fails during a transmission, this is what cuts. For a transceiver whose reading is qualified (IC-7300MK2), the station reads it at each connection and warns you if it is OFF; for the others, it cannot check it.

Safety. An extra safety costs nothing: also set a reasonable maximum power in the transceiver (HF MAX POWER). The station cannot exceed it, whatever happens.

1.4 PC sound

The transceiver's sound card must never be the PC's default sound device, otherwise desktop sounds go to the transceiver (and the desktop occupies the card):

Debian

Settings > Sound: the PC speakers as output, its microphone as input.

Raspberry Pi

Without a desktop (Raspberry Pi OS Lite): nothing to do, no program takes the transceiver's card. With a desktop: as on Debian, in the sound settings of the top bar. The Pi's own sound card (“Headphones”, “HDMI”) is not used by the station.

Windows

Settings > System > Sound: the PC speakers as output, its microphone as input; in “More sound settings”, the transceiver's card is neither “Default Device” nor “Default Communication Device”; sound scheme “No Sounds”. The details are in the Windows notice included in the package.

2. Install the station

Installation never controls the transceiver: it can stay on and connected. Choose your system in the tabs below; the choice applies to the whole page. At the end, openRemoteTRX Console opens and offers the configuration wizard: that is part 3.

Debian

Debian 12 or 13, with a desktop.

  1. Download trx-remote-debian-X.Y.Z.zip (part 0) into Downloads, right-click > “Extract Here”.

  2. In the resulting folder: right-click in an empty area > “Open in Terminal”, then:

    sudo bash install.sh

    Your session password is requested (nothing is displayed while you type: this is normal). The script installs Hamlib, Python, the modules, the service account and the station services.

  3. At the end, log out and log back in (your account's permissions change).
  4. Open openRemoteTRX Console from the applications menu (or type trx-console in a terminal).

Hamlib and recent transceivers. Debian 13 ships Hamlib 4.6, which does not know recent transceivers (IC-7300MK2…): install.sh installs version 4.7 from Debian's official backports by itself. On Debian 12, if your transceiver is missing from rigctl -l, the installation tells you: then run sudo bash compiler-hamlib.sh in the same folder (compiles the latest Hamlib, 10 to 30 minutes).

Without a desktop (server), everything is done over SSH: copy the zip, then

unzip -q trx-remote-debian-*.zip && cd trx-remote-debian-*/ && sudo bash install.sh

close the SSH connection, reconnect and type trx-console.

Raspberry Pi

Raspberry Pi 4 or 5, Raspberry Pi OS 64-bit. The Pi uses the same package as Debian.

The Pi as a station. Already in service with a beta tester, but its load has not yet been measured with everything running (SDR receiver, FreeDV, several visitors): start with the basic functions. A Pi 3 or older is too slow for live audio.

1. Prepare the card, on your PC, with Raspberry Pi Imager (raspberrypi.com/software):

  1. Device: your Pi model. Operating system: Raspberry Pi OS (64-bit) if a screen will stay connected; otherwise Raspberry Pi OS (other) > Raspberry Pi OS Lite (64-bit), without a desktop, everything will be done from your PC.
  2. Customisation, before writing the card: a name (for example trx-pi), an account and its password (required for installing), the time zone and keyboard, Wi-Fi only if there is no cable, and SSH enabled (preferably with a public key).
  3. Write the card, put it in the Pi, connect the network, the transceiver, then the power supply. The first boot takes 2 to 3 minutes (the Pi restarts once by itself).
  4. Find the Pi's address in your router's list of devices, and reserve it (DHCP reservation, part 4.1).

2. Update the system, in a terminal on the Pi (with a desktop) or from your PC (without a desktop: ssh -l <account> <pi-address>):

sudo apt update && sudo apt full-upgrade -y && sudo reboot

3. Install the station.

  • With a desktop: exactly as on Debian (Debian tab): zip downloaded and extracted, sudo bash install.sh in a terminal opened in the folder, then log out and log back in.

  • Without a desktop, from your PC (PowerShell on Windows, terminal on Linux or macOS), in the folder of the downloaded zip:

    scp -o User=<account> trx-remote-debian-X.Y.Z.zip <pi-address>:
ssh -t -l <account> <pi-address>

    then, on the Pi:

    sudo apt install -y unzip
unzip -q trx-remote-debian-X.Y.Z.zip && cd trx-remote-debian-X.Y.Z && sudo bash install.sh

    At the end, type exit and reconnect (ssh -t -l <account> <pi-address>): your account's permissions have changed. Then trx-console.

What the installation does in addition on a Pi.

  • It shows the model and reports a Pi that is too old, too little memory or undervoltage (power supply too weak): fix it before going further.
  • Raspberry Pi OS 13 ships Hamlib 4.6: it installs 4.7 from Debian's official backports (recent transceivers such as the IC-7300MK2). On Raspberry Pi OS 12, if your transceiver is missing from rigctl -l: sudo bash compiler-hamlib.sh (10 to 30 minutes on a Pi).
  • It enables the Pi's hardware watchdog: if the system freezes for more than 15 seconds, the Pi restarts by itself, and the station comes back in receive.
  • With a desktop, it adds the font for openRemoteTRX Console's icons.

4. Check the power supply and the temperature, after a few minutes of running:

vcgencmd get_throttled; vcgencmd measure_temp

Expected: throttled=0x0 and less than 70 °C. Any other value of throttled = power supply too weak or overheating: change the power supply or add a fan. Once the station is running, its supervisor will send you an alert (part 6) if the Pi is slowed down, or if the memory or the disk fill up.

Working without a screen day to day
  • openRemoteTRX Console over SSH: ssh -t -l <account> <pi-address> trx-console (the -t is essential: the console asks questions). Windows Terminal displays the frames, colours and the QR code of menu 4.1 correctly.
  • More comfortable: the console in the browser. Once, menu 4.6 (part 7.5); then https://<pi-address>:8444/ from the PC, the tablet or the phone, without SSH.
  • Dashboard: ssh -t -l <account> <pi-address> trx-monitor, or the vital signs dashboard in the browser (part 7.1).
  • Shut down the Pi: sudo poweroff, then wait until the green LED stops blinking before cutting the power (a sudden power cut can damage the SD card).

Windows

  1. Install Hamlib 64-bit (hamlib-w64-X.Y.Z.exe, version 4.7 or later: recent transceivers such as the IC-7300MK2 do not exist before) from https://github.com/Hamlib/Hamlib/releases, in the suggested folder.
  2. Copy trx-remote-windows-X.Y.Z.zip to the PC. Right-click > Properties > tick Unblock > OK.
  3. Right-click > Extract All, then double-click installer.cmd. If Windows shows “Windows protected your PC”: More info > Run anyway.
  4. Accept the administrator request and answer the questions (Python is installed if it is missing, with your consent).

  5. Essential Windows settings:

    • home network set to the Private profile (the firewall only opens the station on private networks);

4. Using the station on the local network

The local network is your home Wi-Fi or cable: the phone and the station sit behind the same internet box.

Three station pages open in the browser, each one separately, on its own port of the same address:

4.1 A fixed address for the station

In your box, reserve an IP address for the station PC (DHCP reservation). The certificate created by the wizard carries the PC's address: if it changes, the browser and the app will no longer recognise it. After an address change, run the wizard again (menu 2.1) to recreate the certificate.

The address to use is shown at the top of openRemoteTRX Console and by the diagnostic.

4.2 The web page

  1. On the phone or a PC: https://<station-address>:8443
  2. The browser reports an unrecognised certificate: this is expected (certificate created by your station, not by an authority). “Advanced”, then “Proceed”. This is done only once per device.
  3. Log in with the account created by the wizard (Enter in the password field is enough).

Password remembered by the browser. Chrome never saves a password on a page whose certificate is not recognised, even after “Proceed”. The solution: install once, on each device, your station's authority (below). After that, no more warning and the password is remembered.

Install the station authority (once per device)

  1. On the station, openRemoteTRX Console, menu 4.4 “Station certificate”: choice 1 (recreate the certificate, signed by the station authority), then restart the station when the console offers it. The certificate fingerprint changes: pair the Android app again (menu 4.1).
  2. On the device, open the station page: under the password, tap “Install the station certificate” (or open https://<station-address>:8443/station-ca.crt).
  3. Windows: open the downloaded file, “Install Certificate”, “Current User”, “Place all certificates in the following store”, “Trusted Root Certification Authorities”, Finish, then accept the Windows warning. Close all Chrome windows and open it again. Careful: with “Automatically select the certificate store”, Windows puts it in “Personal”, where Chrome ignores it (address still struck through in red): install it again and choose the right store.
  4. Android: Settings, Security (or “Security and privacy”), More security settings, Install from device storage (or “Encryption and credentials”), “CA certificate”, choose the downloaded file and confirm. Open Chrome again.
  5. Open the page: no more warning; log in, Chrome offers to save the password.

This authority can vouch only for private addresses (local network, VPN) and your station's names: even if its key were stolen, it could not impersonate another site (bank…) on your devices.

What you should see: frequency and mode following the transceiver, S-meter, received audio (tap the sound button if the browser blocked it).

To transmit, the page needs the microphone: the browser asks for it at the first transmission. It only offers it over HTTPS, hence the certificate.

Safety. The PTT is a toggle: one press to transmit, one press to go back to receive. The state shown is the one the station confirms, never a guess. STOP cuts at any time. STOP held for 3 seconds (large countdown in the middle of the screen, releasing cancels) opens a window: “Disconnect”, or “Power off the transceiver remotely” (with a confirmation; only if remote power-on is in service, menu 2.8). A hidden page (other tab, locked screen) stops the transmission.

Transceiver off. A red “RIG OFF” banner says so. If remote power-on is in service, your connection powers the transceiver on by itself: a window shows the steps (serial ports checked, command, start-up, read-back) and, if it fails, a clear message with “Retry” and “Continue without the rig”; the “Power on the rig” button of the banner does the same. An automatic reconnection never powers the transceiver on. The app does the same (its STOP cuts as soon as it is pressed); in the car, Android Auto shows “RIG OFF” or “POWERING ON THE RIG…” as the title.

4.3 Several devices, several users

Simple rule: one account per device, named after the device (PC, S25, tablet…), even if they are all yours. The same account opened on a second device closes the connection of the first (and cuts its transmission if it was transmitting). With one account per device, the station knows which one is transmitting, and you can remove a single device (lost phone) without touching the others.

Everything is done in menu 4.3 “Operator accounts” of openRemoteTRX Console:

  • add an account: username, password (Enter = a strong password is generated, write it down), then the pairing QR code of this account for the app;
  • change a password (a forgotten password is replaced: the station does not know it in clear);
  • disable an account (lost or lent phone), then enable it again;
  • delete an account.

The station applies the changes within a few seconds, without restarting; the connection of an account that is disabled, deleted or whose password changed is closed at once. The last active operator account can be neither disabled nor deleted. Only one person can transmit at a time. For listeners without any control, prefer visitors (menu 4.2, part 9).

4.4 The Android app

The app (“openRemoteTRX”, Android 10 or later) brings what the page cannot do: transmission that continues with the screen off, headset button, Android Auto, driving mode.

  1. Install the app (invitation-only testing, part 0).
  2. On the station, menu 4.1: openRemoteTRX Console shows the address, the accounts, the certificate fingerprint and a QR code (without password).
  3. In the app: “Scan the station's QR code”. The address, username and fingerprint are filled in; enter the password.
  4. Log in: same check as for the page (frequency, received audio), then a transmission test on a dummy load with STOP.

The certificate fingerprint protects the link: the app refuses a station whose certificate has changed. After a new certificate (address change), do the pairing again.

Safety. In the car, the phone's touch PTT is hidden (driving mode): use Android Auto, voice commands or the steering-wheel buttons, and obey the highway code.

The station does not manage the network: it listens on an address and a port, and protects access with a username and password. You choose how your phone reaches it from outside. The full technical guide: https://github.com/Bob56fr/openRemoteTRX/blob/main/docs/acces-distant.md

Choose the VPN if in doubt: nothing is exposed, and the audio goes through.

5.2 Option A: VPN

  1. Install the VPN of your choice: server on the box or on the station PC, client on the phone. For Tailscale, a dedicated guide: https://github.com/Bob56fr/openRemoteTRX/blob/main/docs/acces-distant-tailscale.md
  2. One VPN profile per device, never shared. Two devices on the same profile steal the link from each other: dropouts every 15 to 30 seconds.
  3. If the VPN lands on your local network (VPN of the box): nothing to change on the station, use the same address as at home. If the VPN gives the station PC a separate address (Tailscale, WireGuard on the PC): run the wizard again (menu 2.1, network access) so that the certificate also covers this address, then pair the app again (menu 4.1).
  4. On the phone, on 4G, VPN active: open the same page https://<station-address>:8443.

On Android, only one VPN can be active at a time. With Android Auto, route only the station's network through the tunnel (not all traffic), otherwise the car refuses the link: detailed settings in the technical guide, section “Android Auto et VPN”.

5.3 Option B: open port on the box

For those who know their box and certificates.

  1. Check that it is possible: compare the “WAN” address of your box with the one given by a “what is my IP address” website. If they differ, your provider shares your address (CGNAT): no port can be opened, use a VPN.
  2. A domain name pointing to your box (dynamic DNS if the address changes).
  3. A recognised certificate: the simplest is an HTTPS proxy (Caddy) on the station PC, which obtains and renews a Let's Encrypt certificate by itself; the station then keeps listening locally. Example and settings in the technical guide, section “Option B”.
  4. On the box, forward port 443 (TCP) to the station PC.

Safety. With an open port, the password is your only barrier: long and unique for each account. The station slows down attempts (5 failures per minute and per origin, 30 per quarter of an hour and per account), but a stolen password allows transmitting in your allowed bands. The transmission safeguards stay active in all cases, and so does the transceiver's transmit timer (part 1).

5.4 Tests from outside

Phone on 4G (Wi-Fi off), dummy load on the transceiver:

  1. The page opens (option B: padlock without warning).
  2. A wrong password is refused.
  3. The right password connects: frequency, S-meter, received audio.
  4. Transmission on the dummy load: PTT, then STOP; then transmit and switch to airplane mode: back to receive.
  5. If the page opens but the audio does not establish: UDP is not getting through (option B): switch to the VPN.

In case of repeated dropouts, menu 6.4 (network quality) says which segment fails: phone, mobile network, tunnel, box or station.

6. An unattended station

A remotely controlled station runs most of the time with nobody next to it. This part prepares for that.

6.1 Alerts on your phone (ntfy)

The station's supervisor can send you notifications: transmission cut for safety, failure, safety lockout, memory or disk almost full, link dropout, and a silent “Station in service” message every hour (if it stops arriving, the station or its connection has failed).

  1. Install the ntfy app on the phone.
  2. Choose a long topic name that is impossible to guess (for example 20 random letters and digits): on the public ntfy.sh server, anyone who knows the name reads the messages.
  3. Subscribe to this topic in the ntfy app.
  4. On the station, menu 3.5 “Alerts on the phone”: choice 1, paste the address https://ntfy.sh/<your-topic>, save, accept the restart. The screen never shows the full address.
  5. Check: choice 4, “Send a test alert now”; the phone must ring at once (code ORT-8025). After that, the “Station in service” message arrives every hour (choice 3 to change this rhythm).

Each alert carries an “ORT-NNNN” code with a link to its explanation (part 8). Two alerts arrive at restart after a shutdown that was not clean: ORT-8030 (Debian, Raspberry Pi: the machine stopped abruptly, power cut or crash) and ORT-8031 (the supervisor alone stopped); each gives the time of the last sign of life. A normal stop (menu 1.2, PC shutdown) sends nothing.

6.2 The safety lockout

After repeated failures that restarts did not fix, the supervisor puts the station in safety lockout: transceiver in receive, access cut, alert sent. The safety lockout survives restarts: find the cause (menus 6.1 and 6.2), then lift it with menu 1.4.

6.3 After a power cut

ItemTo set
PCin the BIOS / UEFI: “Restore on AC power loss” = Power On (the name varies with the motherboard)

A UPS for the PC, the box and the transceiver's power supply avoids most of these cases.

6.4 Hardware protection

  • Lightning: a station away from any human presence is exposed. Lightning arrester on the antenna cable, and antenna grounded or disconnected when a storm is forecast.
  • Power: the transceiver's ceiling (HF MAX POWER, part 1) remains the last barrier.
  • Transceiver timer (TX TIME OUT TIMER): set to 3 min, it cuts even if everything else has failed.
  • Temperature: the supervisor watches the PC's processor and alerts when it stays above 70 °C for one minute (a peak of a few seconds does not count); the alert says which programs were heating. On Debian and on a Pi, the history of the last 24 h (one reading every 10 s) is attached to the troubleshooting report (menu 6.3). On a Pi, a fan is essential. The transceiver's temperature cannot be read by the station on the transceivers tested (IC-7300MK2, FTDX10): proper ventilation around the transceiver.
  • Local operator: if someone uses the transceiver on site, remote transmission is blocked during their transmission and then for 2 minutes; the station never cuts them for this reason. A transmission with the microphone is cut by the absolute TOT (135 s), unless the operator touched the transceiver (volume, frequency, mode, leaving DATA mode…) in the 2 minutes before speaking or while speaking: the station knows they are present and lets them speak for up to 5 minutes (local_tx_max_s), with a simple alert in between. The transceiver timer, if set shorter, cuts first. These settings are in menu 3.4 “Operator on site”: keep “shared station”; otherwise, a microphone held down leads to the safety lockout.

A refusal is shown in red. “Other answer…” always lets you type your answer if a button is missing. The station's administration password (sudo) is asked once per session, in the panel. The dashboard (1.3) is shown full screen in the page: same sections as in the terminal (radio, SDR receiver if it is in service, audio, connections, health, computer, network with its “Detail” button, log), updated continuously, with the STOP TX button (it can only cut a transmission). Editing station.toml (8.1) is done in a large text field: “Check and save” has the station check the file, show the differences and ask for confirmation before writing; a wrong file is refused in red and nothing is written. “Open in the terminal”, at the top of the panel, replays any menu in the terminal at the bottom of the page. Same page on Debian, on a Raspberry Pi and on Windows.

  • On the station PC: key w in the full screen of openRemoteTRX Console, or trx-console --web in a terminal. The browser opens by itself.
  • From any device on the local network or the VPN (PC, tablet, phone): once, on the station, menu 4.6 “openRemoteTRX Console on the network”: put it in service, then choose the administrator operator accounts (yours). Then open https://<station-address>:8444/ and log in with your usual username and password. Logged in to the control page with an administrator account, you also find the link in the “openRemoteTRX Console (administration)” section, under “Connection”.

Safety. An administrator account opens the station's configuration: strong password, not to be lent. The console can never be reached from the Internet (public addresses refused); after 5 failed attempts, the address is blocked for 15 minutes; each connection sends an alert to the phone (ntfy); the session closes after 30 minutes without action, and a new connection closes the previous one. The station's sudo password is still asked once per session, at the first administration menu.

8.3 Asking for help

  1. Menu 6.3: creates a troubleshooting report (zip on the Desktop, or in the account folder on a Pi without a desktop) without any password (Windows as well as Debian). To read it yourself: menu 6.6, which gives the link to the analyser; drop the zip there and the analysis runs in your browser (the report is sent nowhere).
  2. From the web page: “Report a problem” section (text reviewed before sending).
  3. Open an issue on https://github.com/Bob56fr/openRemoteTRX/issues with the English form (“Report a bug”), describe what you were doing, what you expected, what happened, and attach the report.
  4. Getting help remotely (station-to-station monitoring, menu 6.5, to be enabled first: part 9): the person helping you creates a 10-character code and reads it out to you; type it at choice 4, then compare out loud the 6 digits shown on both sides. Different: answer “no”, the link is erased. Then choose what you send (“Diagnostic” recommended; frequency and mode only if you want). They then see the state of your station and its error codes, masked (no password, no address, no serial number), and can ask you for assistance mode (news every 30 seconds, for a limited time): you are the one who accepts (choice 7). They cannot command anything on your station. You stop everything whenever you want (choice 9). They can also ask you for your troubleshooting report (the one in point 1, fully masked); you can also send it yourself (choice 10). On their side, they get an alert on their phone if your station stops giving news. Away from your station, everything can also be done in the web page, “Monitoring” section: button “Get help: I have a code”, comparison of the 6 digits, choice of what is sent, answer to requests, end.

SDR receiver: protect its input. The SDR cannot withstand the power of your TRX. Separate antenna, or a switch and sequencer that ground the SDR input before any transmission: it is up to you to provide this, according to your equipment. The station cannot do it for you (a transmission with the transceiver microphone is only seen after the fact). The SDR is identified by its serial number: on a machine that has several, never enter the one of an SDR used by another application. Plug it into its own USB port, never into the transceiver's hub. The “USB ports” line of the COMPUTER card (dashboard, menu 1.3) tells you whether a USB bus is overloaded or whether the SDR is slowed down; the details and what to do are in the diagnostic (menu 6.1). A blue socket (USB 3) does not speed up a USB 2 SDR: it shares the 480 Mbit/s of the computer's USB 2 part.

Safety. Retransmitting to third parties what the station receives (visitors, broadcast listeners) makes you responsible, especially outside the amateur radio bands; by default, visitors and listeners hear only silence outside your bands (two independent settings).

Depending on the transceiver, two features of the page may be missing: the transceiver's HF spectrum (only through the second port of an Icom such as the IC-7300MK2; otherwise only the audio spectrum is shown) and the FIL1/2/3 filter presets (specific to Icom).
